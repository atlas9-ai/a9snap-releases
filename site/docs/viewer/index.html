    A9 View

    Double-click any picture or video and A9 Snap shows it at once, in a window the size of the picture, with the rest of the folder one wheel-click away. Zoom with a small map of where you are, turn a picture, play a video, save a frame of it or cut its start and end off (Pro), see the folder as thumbnails, read its info, rename it, then mark it up in the same window, make it ready to send (smaller, another type, no location), copy it or send it. It is free, it writes nothing about what you look at, and it never makes itself your default app.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.19.0

    A9 View is the part of A9 Snap that looks at pictures and plays videos. It is for files that did not come from A9 Snap too: a photo from your phone, a download, a logo, a diagram, a screen recording. It opens quickly, shows the picture at its own size, and puts the same tools you use on captures one key away. It is part of the free app, and it lives inside it: one install, one icon in the tray. Videos are described in Videos below.

    Open a picture#

    First choose A9 Snap in Windows (see Choose A9 Snap in Windows below), then double-click a picture or a video in Explorer. You can also right-click one and choose Open with, then A9 Snap, without choosing anything first.

    • If A9 Snap is already running, it shows the picture in the A9 View window at once. If it is not, it starts, shows the picture first and then finishes starting up, and stays in the tray as after any start.
    • Select several pictures and press Enter: A9 View shows the first one.
    • Inside A9 View, press Ctrl+O (or the Open button) for a file dialog, or drop a picture, a video or a folder on the window. A folder opens at its first item.
    • One window is used for everything. Opening another picture replaces the one on screen. Closing the window hides it; it is kept ready for the next one.

    A9 Snap opens PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, ICO, SVG, TIFF and HEIC pictures. An animated GIF or WebP plays. An SVG is shown as a picture only: nothing in it runs. A photo's stored rotation is respected, so a phone picture is upright. TIFF and HEIC are read by Windows itself; if your PC has no decoder for a type, the window says which type it cannot open and what Windows needs (for HEIC, the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store), and the wheel still moves to the next picture. A picture that cannot be read (damaged, locked or gone) shows its name and the reason in the window.

    Size and place#

    By default the window fits the picture. A picture smaller than your screen opens at 100%, never scaled up: one picture pixel is one screen pixel, so it is sharp at any display scale. A bigger one is scaled down to fit inside 90% of the screen's usable area. The window opens in the middle of the monitor your pointer is on, and never smaller than its title bar needs.

    Change this with A9 View window in Settings: Fit each picture (the default) or Keep my size and place, where the window stays where and how big you left it and each picture fits inside it.

    Mouse wheel in A9 View in Settings swaps the wheel and Ctrl+wheel, if you would rather zoom with the wheel alone.

    The title bar shows the file name, where it is in the folder ("12 of 48"), its size in pixels and on disk, and the zoom. Nothing else is on screen until you move the pointer: then the toolbar and arrows appear.

    The mini-map#

    When the picture is bigger than the window shows, a small overview of the whole picture appears at the bottom right, with a rectangle for the part you are looking at. Click or drag in it to move around. The cross closes it until your next zoom, and it hides by itself when the whole picture is in view. It never covers the toolbar. Mini-map in Settings turns it off for good.

    The toolbar#

    Move the pointer over the window and a bar appears at the bottom, then fades after a moment. It holds Open, Zoom out, Zoom in, Fit or 100%, Previous, Next, Rotate left, Rotate right, Flip, Edit, Ready to send, Stay on top, Info, Thumbnails and A9 View settings (which opens Settings on this section). Every button's tooltip names its key. Right-click Previous or Next to jump to the first or last picture, or back or forward 10.

    Toolbar in Settings keeps the bar always shown, or never shown.

    Videos#

    A9 View plays MP4, M4V, MOV and WebM videos in the same window, with the same wheel, arrows, zoom, full screen, thumbnails, info, rename and Delete. An animated GIF stays a picture. Nothing here changes or overwrites a video file; Trim (Pro) writes a new one.

    • Opening. Double-click a video in Explorer (once you have chosen A9 Snap for that type), drop one on the window, press Ctrl+O, or start A9 Snap with its path. A video opened this way plays at once. A video you arrive at by moving through the folder shows its first frame and a play button and waits, unless Play videos as I move through a folder is on in Settings.
    • A9 Snap's own recordings open here too: from the Home window, the strip and the note after a recording. Open my recordings in A9 View in Settings (on by default) switches it; off, they open in the app Windows uses for them.
    • The window opens at the video's own pixel size under the same rule as pictures (Fit each picture or Keep my size and place). Ctrl+wheel zoom, dragging and the mini-map work on a video as on a picture. Rotate and flip do nothing on a video.
    • A video is read from disk as it plays, a piece at a time, never loaded whole: a two-hour or multi-gigabyte recording opens as fast as a short one.

    Controls#

    A row of controls sits just above the toolbar and shows and hides with it: play and pause, a scrub bar with the time so far and the length (drag it and the picture follows), volume and mute, speed (0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 times) and loop.

    Do thisWhat happens
    Click the video, or SpacePlays and pauses. At the end it stops on the last frame, unless loop is on.
    Shift+Left / Shift+RightJumps 5 seconds back or forward.
    , / .Steps one frame back or forward (the video pauses first).
    MMutes and unmutes.
    SSave this frame (below).
    Left / Right and the wheelStill the previous and next item in the folder, so you are never stuck on a video.

    The volume and the mute switch are remembered between sessions. The speed is 1 each time A9 Snap starts.

    Save this frame#

    S, the camera button on the toolbar, or Save this frame in the right-click menu saves the frame on screen as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder (named like any capture) and copies it. It is the whole video frame at the video's own pixel size, not a picture of the window. It is free.

    What a video shows#

    The title bar adds the video's length ("clip.mp4 · 3 of 9 · 800 × 450 · 0:05 · 1.2 MB · 100%"). In the thumbnail strip a video shows a frame with a small play mark, made in memory like the rest and never saved. The info panel (I) adds the length, frame rate, video format and audio format, and whether the video has sound. The right-click menu has Save this frame, Trim (Pro), Copy (the file), Share, Show in folder and Delete; the text and picture tools are for pictures.

    Trim (Pro)#

    Trim cuts the start and the end off a video and saves the part you keep as a new file. It is part of A9 Snap Pro: everyone sees the Trim button (it carries a small PRO badge without Pro), and using it without Pro opens the Pro sheet and writes nothing.

    1. Press E on a video, or press Trim on the toolbar or in the right-click menu. Two handles appear on the scrub bar, the part outside them is dimmed, and the whole video is kept to begin with.
    2. Drag the handles to the first and last moment you want to keep. A handle can't cross the other one, and the shortest part is half a second. Or play or seek to a moment and press [ to start the kept part there, or ] to end it there.
    3. Play plays only the kept part and stops at its end.
    4. Press Save trimmed video. A progress bar shows while it writes; Cancel stops it and leaves no file. When it is done, the new file is shown in the window.

    The new file is the next numbered recording in your screenshots folder (named like any recording, so the strip and Home find it), it keeps the sound, and it starts and ends within a tenth of a second of the points you chose. The original is never changed or replaced. Trim writes the new picture with the same encoder A9 Snap's recorder uses (the PC's hardware encoder when it has one, Microsoft's otherwise), so the new file looks like the old one but is made again, not copied; a long video takes a little while. The picture is read at whatever size the video is (a width that is not a multiple of 16 included); if A9 Snap can't read how a video's picture is stored, Trim says so and writes nothing. Press Esc to leave Trim without saving.

    Trim works on MP4, M4V and MOV videos holding H.264 picture and AAC sound (or none), which is what A9 Snap records. For any other file, for example a WebM or an MP4 in another format, the button is greyed out and its tooltip says why in one line.

    A video A9 View can't play#

    A file with a video ending that is not a video, or a format the built-in player does not know, shows A9 View can't play this video with Open with another app, which asks Windows for its Open with list for that file. The wheel and the arrows still move on to the other items. A9 View does not promise to play every format.

    Stopping#

    Playback stops, and the file is released, when you move to another item, close the window or A9 View hides. After closing, no sound goes on and you can rename, move or delete the file at once. Delete and F2 work on a video as on a picture.

    Rotate and flip#

    R turns the picture a quarter turn to the right and Shift+R to the left. H flips it left to right and V top to bottom. This is only a view: the file is never changed, and the picture goes back to normal when you move to another one. The mini-map, zooming at the pointer and dragging follow the turned picture.

    To keep the turned picture, right-click and choose Save a copy. It is written as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, named like any capture, and copied. The original stays exactly as it was. Dragging the picture out into another app, and Copy, give the file as it is on disk (unless you saved a copy).

    Thumbnails#

    G (or the Thumbnails button) shows a row of thumbnails of the folder along the bottom, with the current picture marked. Click one to jump to it; the wheel over the strip scrolls it, and it follows you as you move. Ctrl+click picks or unpicks a thumbnail and Shift+click picks a run of them, for a batch; a plain click clears the picks. Right-click a thumbnail to show it or to compare it with the picture on screen. The thumbnails are made after the picture is on screen, held in memory only and never saved anywhere. The strip is off to begin with, and A9 View remembers if you turn it on.

    Info#

    I opens a panel at the side with the picture's name, folder, type, size on disk, pixel size, and modified and created dates. When the file carries them it also shows the camera, lens, exposure, date taken and location. A value can be copied with the button beside it. The panel only reads; it edits nothing.

    A location shows as coordinates with Open in maps, which opens a map in your browser. That click is the only way anything about a picture leaves your PC: A9 Snap builds the link from the picture's own coordinates, and sends nothing until you click. See Privacy.

    Stay on top#

    T keeps the A9 View window above your other windows until you turn it off or close the window. A pin shows in the title bar while it is on.

    Rename#

    F2 renames the picture in its folder. A box opens at the top of the window with the name (without its ending); Enter renames and Esc cancels. The ending stays as it is: renaming never converts a picture. A name that is already taken, or that Windows does not allow, says why and changes nothing. Renaming a capture takes its edit original, its note of where it came from and its searchable text with it, and its number stays taken. Because a capture is found by its numbered name, a capture renamed to something else no longer shows in the strip or on Home.

    Order and looping#

    Items come in name order, the way Explorer sorts names: 2 comes before 10. Right-click the picture, or open Settings, to sort by name, date modified or size, ascending or descending. Loop at the ends (on by default) makes the next item after the last the first, and the other way round; turn it off and the wheel stops at the ends. Pictures and videos are items of the same folder and share one count and one order; Include videos when moving through a folder in Settings leaves the videos out. The next and previous pictures are read ahead, so a step shows at once.

    Transparent pictures#

    Transparent pictures in Settings chooses what shows behind the see-through parts of a picture: a checkerboard (the default) or the dark ground of the window.

    The right-click menu#

    Right-click the picture for Edit (in this window), Ready to send, Compare with… (Pro), Copy, Copy text, Redact, Share, Show in folder and Delete, then Save a copy while the picture is turned, and the folder's order and Loop at the ends. On a video the menu is Save this frame, Trim (Pro), Copy, Share, Show in folder and Delete, then the same order and Loop at the ends. Copy text, Redact and Share are part of A9 Snap Pro: without it they carry a small PRO badge and open the Pro sheet. Redact copies the picture with its email addresses, phone numbers and card numbers blurred; the file is not changed. See Auto-redact.

    You can also drag the picture out of the window and drop the file into another app, as in the strip. While you are zoomed in, a drag moves the picture instead; hold Alt to carry the file out.

    Edit a picture#

    Press E on a picture (or click Edit on the toolbar, or choose it in the right-click menu) and the editor's tools come up in the same window, on the picture as it is shown: arrow, box, ellipse, highlight, text, numbered steps, stamps, blur, crop, Redact (Pro), Shadow and Copy text, with the same keys and the same look. Your place in the folder, and the thumbnail strip if it is on, stay where they were. A window fitted to a small picture grows to make room for the tools. If the picture is turned or flipped when you press E, editing starts from it as shown and a save writes it as shown. A video is not edited: E on a video is Trim.

    Press Esc or E again to leave. Because E leaves, the Ellipse tool is O here (in the editor window it is still E). The wheel and Left and Right still move to the other pictures, and Ctrl+wheel zooms, Space and drag (or the middle button) moves around, and the mini-map follows. Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo for the picture you are editing, and Ctrl+C copies the picture with your edits. The Drag file handle drags the file as it is on disk: your edits are in it only after you save.

    What saving does#

    • A capture (a numbered file A9 Snap took, in your screenshots folder): Ctrl+S or Save saves over it, keeps the untouched original in the hidden .a9snap folder, copies the result, and Revert puts the original back, exactly as in the editor window.
    • Any other picture is never changed. Ctrl+S is Save a copy: it writes name (edited).jpg next to the picture, then name (edited 2).jpg and so on, and never overwrites anything. The copy is in the same format when A9 Snap can write it (PNG, JPG at high quality, WebP, BMP). HEIC, SVG, GIF, TIFF, AVIF and ICO are saved as a PNG (a GIF keeps only its first frame), and a line above the picture says so before you save. An animated WebP or PNG also keeps only its first frame, and says so. A copy carries no camera or location data.
    • Save as new capture (Ctrl+Shift+S) is there for every picture: it writes the edited picture as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder and leaves the first picture alone.
    • If the folder can't be written to (a read-only folder, a drive you can't change), A9 View says so, writes nothing, keeps your edits and offers Save as new capture instead.
    • After a save the window shows the file that was saved.

    Unsaved edits are asked about#

    Moving to another picture (Left, Right, the wheel, a thumbnail), closing the window, Ctrl+O, dropping a file on the window or opening another picture from Explorer asks Save, Don't save or Cancel. Save uses the save that fits the picture (over a capture, a copy beside anything else); Cancel keeps every edit and stays where you are.

    E edits in A9 View in Settings (on by default) switches this. Off, E opens the editor window instead, on a copy for a picture that is not a capture, as in 0.16. The editor window itself, which opens after a capture, from Home and from the strip, is unchanged.

    Ready to send#

    A picture that is about to leave your PC often needs one more step: it is too big for an email or a ticket, a form wants another type, or a phone photo carries where it was taken. Press P (or Ready to send on the toolbar or in the right-click menu) and a panel opens beside the picture. Everything happens on your PC, the original is never changed, and it is free.

    The panel shows the original's size in pixels and on disk, and under it the result's, worked out as you choose (for a very large WebP it shows about how big at once, and the exact size a moment later):

    • Size. Long edge (3840, 2560, 1920, 1280, 800, or a number you type), Percent (75, 50, 25, or a number), or Original. The shape is kept. A ready-made size never makes a picture bigger; a number you type can. A result is at most 16,384 pixels a side.
    • Type. Keep as is, PNG, JPG or WebP. JPG and WebP have a Quality slider (85 to begin with). Keep as is keeps a PNG, JPG or WebP; a GIF, BMP, TIFF, HEIC, AVIF or ICO becomes a PNG, and a line says so. See-through areas become white in a JPG, with a note. An animated picture gives its first frame only. An SVG can't be made ready.
    • Make it smaller. Under 1 MB, Under 500 KB, Under 200 KB, or Off. A9 View lowers the quality first (never below 40) and makes the picture smaller only if it has to, then says what it chose. If it can't get under the limit, it says that, with the smallest it could make. 1 MB here is 1,000,000 bytes.
    • Remove location and camera details (on to begin with). The line under it lists what the file carries: location, camera, lens, date taken and other tags (such as software, owner, comments and notes), or None found. A photo's stored rotation is applied to the pixels first, so nothing turns sideways. The colour profile stays, because without it the colours would change. Turn this off to keep the camera's own details; the line says which can't be carried over.

    Where the result goes#

    • Save beside the original writes name (ready).jpg next to the picture, then name (ready 2).jpg and so on. Nothing is ever overwritten. The window then shows the new file.
    • Save as new capture writes the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder. A capture is always a PNG, so with Make it smaller on, the picture is made smaller until the PNG fits.
    • Copy puts the result on the clipboard.

    If the folder can't be written to, A9 View says so, writes nothing and offers Save as new capture. The choices you last saved with are remembered. Esc or P closes the panel.

    JPG files are written with code based in part on the work of the Independent JPEG Group.

    Batch and side by side (Pro)#

    Doing one picture at a time is free. Many at once and two side by side are part of A9 Snap Pro: everyone sees them with a PRO badge, and using one without Pro opens the Pro sheet and writes nothing.

    Many pictures at once#

    1. Open the panel (P) and choose the size, type and the rest as for one picture.
    2. Under Make many ready, choose the pictures you picked in the thumbnail strip (Ctrl+click, Shift+click) or All pictures in this folder.
    3. Press Make N pictures ready. A progress bar shows how far it is. You can keep looking around while it works.

    The results go in a ready folder inside the folder you are looking at. Each keeps its name, with the new ending; a name that is already there gets (2), and nothing is overwritten. Cancel keeps the files already finished and leaves no half-written one. At the end a summary says how many were made, their total size before and after, and what was skipped and why: videos (never changed), pictures that couldn't be read, and, with Keep as is, types A9 Snap can't write (GIF, BMP, TIFF, HEIC, AVIF, ICO and SVG). Choose PNG, JPG or WebP to include all of them but SVG. Show the ready folder opens it.

    Side by side#

    Right-click the picture and choose Compare with…, or right-click a thumbnail and choose Compare with this picture. The picture on screen is shown next to the other one (the next in the folder to begin with, or the other of two picked thumbnails). Zoom and place move together: the wheel zooms both, a drag moves both, Ctrl+0 fits them and Ctrl+1 shows the first at 100%. Click a thumbnail, or press Left or Right, to change the other picture. Swap sides changes which is on the left.

    Save side by side writes one picture holding both, as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder: a small gap between them, on the window's dark ground (behind a see-through picture, what Transparent pictures is set to), with a label over each (Before and After to begin with; type your own, or untick Labels). A pair too big for one picture is scaled down together. The window then shows the saved picture, and Ready to send works on it like any other. Esc closes the comparison.

    Choose A9 Snap in Windows#

    Windows lets only you decide which app opens your pictures and videos, so A9 Snap never makes itself the default. What it does is tell Windows that it can open these types, so it appears under Open with and in Windows' Default apps list. Installing A9 Snap changes nothing about what opens your pictures or videos today, and uninstalling removes what it added.

    In Settings, the A9 View section has Choose A9 Snap in Windows. It opens Windows' Default apps page for A9 Snap, where you pick the types you want it to open, and it lists the types that open with A9 Snap now: the picture types on one line and the video types on their own line, so nobody gets video by surprise. On the Microsoft Store version A9 Snap offers the same types through its package.

    Privacy#

    A9 View reads the picture or video and the names of the other files in its folder, and writes nothing: no thumbnails on disk, no frames, no list of what you viewed, no index. The only files it can write are ones you ask for (Save a copy, Save this frame, Save trimmed video, saving an edit, Ready to send, the ready folder of a batch, or Save side by side). Ready to send and side by side work on your PC: nothing is uploaded, and no list of the pictures you made ready is kept. Editing a picture that is not a capture writes only the copy you save: no kept original, no thumbnail, no edit mark and no search text; the copy has no camera or location data. A video is read through A9 Snap's own local route, a piece at a time, and no network is involved; nothing is held open between pieces, so the file is free the moment it stops playing. The log says the type, the sizes and the times, never a file or folder name. Secure captures and the secure vault are never opened by A9 View. It makes no network call; the one thing that leaves your PC is the map link you open yourself from the info panel.
