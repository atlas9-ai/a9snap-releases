    What's new

    What changed in each version of A9 Snap, newest first.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.21.0

    0.21.0 · November 27, 2026#

    • Click highlights in recordings (Pro). A recording now shows where you clicked: a ring appears around the pointer at each press and fades in about half a second. Left and right clicks have their own colours (yellow and blue to start), a double-click shows two quick rings, and holding a button down keeps a smaller ring on the pointer until you let go, so drags are visible.
    • Only in the video. The rings are drawn into the recorded frames, never on your screen, so they stay out of your way and out of other apps. Only clicks inside the recorded area are shown, not clicks on the recording bar or while paused. They work with region, window and full-screen recording, every display scale, pause and resume, GIFs made from a recording and trimmed videos.
    • Settings has a new Recording section with Highlight clicks, Left-click colour, Right-click colour, Ring size (Small, Medium, Large) and a small live preview. Show the pointer in recordings and Longest recording moved there from Saving.
    • Nothing changes under you. Highlights are on for a new install. If you recorded with an earlier version they start off, and the note after your next recording says where to turn them on.
    • Privacy. A9 Snap watches the mouse only while such a recording runs, uses only where and when you clicked inside the recorded area, writes that nowhere but into the video, and captures no keys. The log says only whether highlights were on.
    • Pro. Click highlights in recordings is in Settings › Pro, the Pro sheet, a9snap.com and the manual's Pro page, in the same words. It is part of recording, which was already Pro.
    • Manual. The Record video page has Click highlights; Settings and Privacy cover them.
    • Tests. Unit tests cover the ring's timing and fade, the two colours, double-clicks, held buttons, sizes at 100, 125 and 150 %, clicks outside the area and the start for people who already record; scripts/e2e/clicks.mjs records on the real app with scripted clicks and reads the saved video and GIF back.

    0.20.0 · November 26, 2026#

    • Custom redact patterns (Pro). Teach Auto-redact your own private text. Settings has a new Auto-redact section (the two Auto-redact rows moved there from Saving) with My patterns: each has a name, an on/off switch and either a Simple shape (# any digit, A any letter, * any letter or digit, everything else itself, such as EMP-######; \#, \A and \* mean the characters) or an Advanced regular expression, with Ignore case (on) and Allow spaces or dashes inside (off). Add a starter offers ticket numbers, employee IDs and account numbers. An expression that isn't valid says why and can't be saved.
    • Words to always hide. One word or phrase per line, matched as whole words, ignoring case.
    • Try it. A box that shows, as you type, which patterns and words match and what they would hide, the pattern you are editing included.
    • Everywhere Auto-redact runs. Matches are blurred like the built-in ones, on every new capture when that is switched on, with Redact in the editor and A9 View's editing mode, and with Copy redacted. The editor now shows a Hidden list that names what found each blur (a built-in kind, your pattern's name or the word list), with Undo beside each, so a wrong match is quick to spot and take back.
    • Share with a team. Export saves your patterns and words to a .a9redact file (plain JSON); Import lists a file first, then adds what is new and skips exact duplicates. Nothing is sent anywhere.
    • Limits. Up to 100 patterns and 500 words. Each pattern gets 50 ms per picture; one that runs over is skipped for that picture, the note after a capture and in the editor says so, and the list marks it Too slow.
    • Pro. Custom redact patterns is a feature in Settings › Pro, in the Pro sheet, on a9snap.com and in the manual's Pro page, in the same words. Without Pro the section shows the PRO badge, pressing it opens the Pro sheet, and nothing is saved.
    • Privacy. Patterns and words stay in your settings on this PC; the log records only how many, never a pattern, a word or what matched.
    • Manual. The Auto-redact page has My patterns, starters, words, Try it, sharing and limits; Settings and Privacy cover them.
    • Tests. Unit tests cover the Simple shapes and escaping, Ignore case, spaces and dashes, whole words, the starters, import and duplicates, and a deliberately slow expression; scripts/e2e/redact-patterns.mjs drives the real app and scripts/e2e/redact-speed.mjs times it.

    0.19.0 · November 26, 2026#

    • Ready to send, in A9 View (free). Press P (or Ready to send on the toolbar or in the right-click menu) and a panel opens beside the picture: resize by the long edge (3840, 2560, 1920, 1280, 800 or a number you type) or by percent, keeping the shape and never making a picture bigger unless you type a bigger number; keep the type or write a PNG, JPG or WebP, with a quality slider (85 to start); Make it smaller (under 1 MB, 500 KB or 200 KB), which lowers the quality first and shrinks the picture only if it has to, and says what it chose; and Remove location and camera details (on to start), which lists what the file carries. The result's size in pixels and on disk shows next to the original's as you choose (a very large WebP shows an "about" size at once and the exact one a moment later). Everything happens on your PC and the original is never changed.
    • Where it goes. Save beside the original as name (ready).jpg (then (ready 2)), Save as new capture (a capture is always a PNG), or Copy. A folder that can't be written to says so and offers Save as new capture. Your last choices are remembered, and the view shows the saved file.
    • Details and colour. A photo's stored rotation is applied to the picture before its details are removed, so nothing turns sideways. The colour profile is kept, because without it colours would change. See-through areas become white in a JPG, with a note; an animated picture gives its first frame, with a note; a type A9 Snap can't write (such as GIF or HEIC) becomes a PNG with a note.
    • Batch (Pro). Pick pictures in the thumbnail strip with Ctrl+click or Shift+click, or take All pictures in this folder, and make them all ready at once into a ready folder next to them. Names are kept, nothing is overwritten ( (2)), a progress bar has Cancel (finished files stay, no half file is left), and a summary says how many, the size before and after, and what was skipped and why. Videos are never changed.
    • Side by side (Pro). Compare with… shows the picture next to another one from the folder, zoom and place moving together. Save side by side writes both as the next numbered capture with a small gap and labels (Before and After, editable), ready to go through Ready to send like any picture.
    • Pro. Batch send-ready and side by side is the twelfth feature in Settings › Pro, in the Pro sheet, on a9snap.com and in the manual's Pro page, in the same words. Both carry the PRO badge, and without Pro they open the Pro sheet and write nothing. Ready to send for one picture is free.
    • Rotated photos are quick. A phone photo stored on its side now gets its thumbnail in A9 View's strip in under a second. Before, each one could take more than ten seconds.
    • Manual and privacy. The A9 View page has Ready to send, batch and side by side sections; the privacy page says what Remove location and camera details removes and that it happens on your PC; the keyboard shortcuts list P. JPG files are written with code based in part on the work of the Independent JPEG Group (THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
    • Tests. Unit tests cover the resize sizes, the (ready N) and batch names, the size search, what removal lists and takes away, rotation before removal, the white fill for JPG and the colour profile; scripts/e2e/ready.mjs drives the real app and scripts/e2e/ready-speed.mjs times it.

    0.18.0 · November 26, 2026#

    • Backgrounds in the editor (Pro). Click Background in the editor's toolbar to put the picture on a backdrop: 0 to 200 pixels of padding (64 to start), a solid colour, one of eight gradients, your own two-colour gradient or none (see-through), rounded corners of 0 to 40 pixels (12 to start), a Soft, Strong or no shadow, and a shape of Auto (the same room on every side), 16:9, 4:3 or 1:1 with the picture in the middle. The picture on screen changes as you move each setting, and dragging a slider is one undo step. Click Background again to take it off. It works the same in the editor window and in A9 View's editing mode.
    • Share-ready. One click puts the picture on the background you used last, or the standard one the first time. The last style is remembered between sessions, in settings.json, on this PC only.
    • Crop first, then the background. The backdrop wraps the cropped picture, and your arrows, text and blurs stay on the picture inside it. While the Crop tool is on, the background steps aside so you can see what you are cutting. With a background on, the plain Shadow button is off, because the background has its own shadow.
    • What is saved. The background is in the saved file, in the copy on the clipboard and in anything you share or drag out afterwards; the footer says the size the file will have. A capture still keeps its untouched original, so Revert brings back the plain picture, and any other picture in A9 View still gets a (edited) copy beside it. A backdrop of None keeps its see-through corners and margin in a PNG; saving a JPG or BMP says in one line that they are filled with white, and fills them. A picture too big for a canvas (over 16,000 pixels a side) says so and is left as it was. In A9 View the mini-map is hidden while a background is on.
    • Pro. Backgrounds is the eleventh feature in Settings › Pro, in the Pro sheet, on a9snap.com and in the manual's Pro page, in the same words. Without Pro, Background and Share-ready carry a PRO badge, and clicking either opens the Pro sheet and changes nothing.
    • Manual and privacy. The editor page has a Backgrounds section with a picture, and the privacy page says what is kept (the last style, in your settings) and that nothing is sent anywhere. There are no new keyboard shortcuts.
    • Tests. Unit tests cover the size for each padding and shape, corners and shadow at 100, 125 and 150 % scaling, the JPG and BMP fill, crop then background, the remembered style and undo of a slider drag; scripts/e2e/background.mjs drives the real app.

    0.17.0 · November 25, 2026#

    • Edit pictures inside A9 View. Press E on a picture (or the new Edit button on the toolbar, or Edit in the right-click menu) and the editor's tools come up in the same window, on the picture as shown (turned or flipped included). Same tools, keys and look as the editor: arrow, box, ellipse, highlight, text, numbered steps, stamps, blur, crop, Redact (Pro), Shadow and Copy text. Esc or E again leaves. The folder, the thumbnail strip and your place in it stay where they were; a window fitted to a small picture grows to make room for the tools.
    • Any picture, safely. A picture A9 Snap took is saved the way the editor window saves it: Ctrl+S saves over it, keeps the untouched original and Revert works. Any other picture is never changed: Ctrl+S is Save a copy, which writes name (edited).jpg next to it (then (edited 2), (edited 3)), in the same format when A9 Snap can write it (PNG, JPG, WebP, BMP) and as a PNG otherwise, with a line before saving that says so (HEIC, SVG, GIF, TIFF, AVIF, ICO); an animated picture keeps its first frame, and the line says that too. A copy carries no camera or location data. If the folder can't be written to, it says so, changes nothing and offers Save as new capture instead; that button, the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, is there for every picture (Ctrl+Shift+S). After a save the window shows the saved file.
    • Unsaved edits are never lost by accident. Moving to another picture (Left, Right, the wheel, a thumbnail), closing the window, Ctrl+O, dropping a file or opening another picture from Explorer asks Save, Don't save or Cancel; Cancel keeps every edit.
    • Zoom and pan keep working. Ctrl+wheel zooms, Space and drag (or the middle button) pans, and the mini-map follows. The plain wheel and Left and Right still move to other pictures, asking first when there are unsaved edits. Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo for the picture you are editing; Ctrl+C copies the picture with its edits; dragging the file out drags the file on disk (its tooltip says your edits are in it only after you save).
    • Two keys differ from the editor window. In A9 View E leaves editing, so the Ellipse tool is O there. The editor window is unchanged and still opens after a capture, from Home and from the strip.
    • Setting. E edits in A9 View (A9 View, Editing; on by default). Off, E opens the editor window as in 0.16.
    • Nothing extra is written. For a picture that isn't a capture, the only thing written is the file you save: no kept original, no thumbnail, no edit state, no search text. The log never names a file and nothing is sent anywhere. The editor's code loads when you first press E, so opening a picture and stepping through a folder are not slower.

    0.16.1 · November 25, 2026#

    • Fixed: Trim made a diagonal smear of most recordings. A video whose width is not a multiple of 16 (nearly every recording of a region you drag, for example 780 wide) came out of Trim with the right length and sound but a picture sheared into diagonal streaks, because each decoded row was read as exactly as wide as the picture while the decoder pads its rows. Trim now reads the row width and layout the decoder reports for every frame, for any width and height. The original was never touched; trim the recording again from the original.
    • The same fix for a video's thumbnail and Save as GIF. They read frames the same way, so the strip's thumbnail for such a video and a GIF made from it were sheared too; both are right now. Save this frame was never affected (the player draws that picture).
    • A frame layout A9 Snap can't read stops the job with a plain message ("A9 Snap can't read how this video's picture is stored, so nothing was made.") and writes no file: it never saves a video it can't vouch for.
    • Tests. A new end-to-end script, scripts/e2e/trim-picture.mjs, trims videos at 780 x 1080, 1278 x 718, 642 x 362 and 1280 x 720 (made by ffmpeg and recorded by A9 Snap) and compares the pictures with the original's by SSIM, along with Save this frame and the strip's thumbnail; the 0.16.0 tests only checked length and sound, and used 1280 wide, where the decoder's rows are as wide as the picture.

    0.16.0 · November 25, 2026#

    • A9 View plays videos. MP4, M4V, MOV and WebM files open in the same window as pictures, and count as items of the folder next to them: the "12 of 48" in the title, the wheel, the arrows, Home and End, the order and Loop at the ends all include them. Animated GIFs stay pictures. A video you open yourself (a double-click once you have chosen A9 Snap for the type, a drop, Ctrl+O, a path on the command line) plays at once; one you arrive at by moving through the folder shows its first frame and a play button and waits. The window opens at the video's own size under the same rule as pictures, and Ctrl+wheel zoom, dragging and the mini-map work on it. Rotate and flip do nothing on a video.
    • Controls. A row above the toolbar has play and pause, a scrub bar with the time so far and the length (drag it and the picture follows), volume and mute, speed (0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 times) and loop. Space or a click plays and pauses, Shift+Left and Shift+Right jump 5 seconds, , and . step one frame, M mutes, Left, Right and the wheel still move to the previous and next item so nobody is stuck on a video. At the end it stops on the last frame unless Loop is on. Volume and mute are remembered; the speed is 1 each time A9 Snap starts.
    • Save this frame (free). S, the toolbar's camera button or the right-click menu saves the frame on screen as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder (named like any capture) and copies it, at the video's own pixel size.
    • Trim (Pro). E on a video, or the Trim button on the toolbar or in the right-click menu, opens Trim in the same window: two handles on the scrub bar mark the start and the end, the part outside is dimmed, and Play plays only the kept part ([ and ] set the start and the end where the video is). Save trimmed video writes the part as a new recording, the next number in your screenshots folder, with its sound, and then shows it; it starts and ends within a tenth of a second of the points you chose, and the original is never changed. A progress bar shows while it writes and Cancel leaves no file. It cuts with the encoder A9 Snap's recorder uses (no encoder program is added to the installer), so it works on MP4, M4V and MOV videos holding H.264 picture and AAC sound or none, which is what A9 Snap records; for a WebM or another format the button is greyed out and its tooltip says why. Trim is visible to everyone with the PRO badge; without Pro it opens the Pro sheet and writes nothing.
    • A9 Snap's own recordings open here. Clicking a recording or a GIF on the Home page, Enter or a double-click in the strip, and the note after a recording open it in A9 View. Open my recordings in A9 View in Settings (on by default) switches it; off, they open in the app Windows uses for them.
    • What a video shows. The title bar adds its length. The thumbnail strip shows a frame with a small play mark (made in memory, never saved). The info panel adds the length, frame rate, video and audio format, and whether it has sound.
    • A video A9 View can't play (a file with a video ending that isn't a video, or a format the built-in player doesn't know) shows A9 View can't play this video with Open with another app; the wheel and the arrows still move on.
    • Read as it plays, let go the moment it stops. A video is read from disk a piece at a time through the app's own local route, never loaded whole, so a two-hour or multi-gigabyte recording opens as fast as a short one. Playback stops and the file is released when you move to another item, close the window or A9 View hides; after closing, no sound goes on and the file can be renamed, moved or deleted at once. Nothing about a video is written anywhere, no network is used, and the log never names a file.
    • Settings. The A9 View section has a new Video group: Include videos when moving through a folder, Play videos as I move through a folder and Open my recordings in A9 View. Choose A9 Snap in Windows lists the video types on their own line, under the pictures', and the installer registers them under a class of its own so nobody gets video by surprise. The manual's A9 View, Keyboard shortcuts, Settings and Privacy pages, the strip and recording pages and the README say so.

    0.15.0 · November 24, 2026#

    • The viewer is now A9 View. The window, the Settings section (was Viewer) and the manual page say A9 View. It is still part of A9 Snap (one install, one tray icon) and still free. Pictures from anywhere open in it as before.
    • A mini-map when you are zoomed in. While the picture is bigger than the window, a small overview of the whole picture sits at the bottom right with a rectangle for the part in view; click or drag in it to move around. The cross closes it until your next zoom, it hides when the whole picture is in view, and it never covers the toolbar. Mini-map in Settings turns it off.
    • A toolbar. Move the pointer over the window and a bar appears at the bottom, then fades: Open, Zoom out, Zoom in, Fit or 100%, Previous, Next, Rotate left, Rotate right, Flip, Stay on top, Info, Thumbnails and A9 View settings. Each tooltip names its key; right-click Previous or Next for the first or last picture, or back or forward 10. Toolbar in Settings keeps it always shown or never shown.
    • Rotate and flip. R and Shift+R turn the picture right and left, H and V flip it. It is a view only: the file is never changed and the picture goes back to normal when you move on. Save a copy in the right-click menu writes the turned picture as the next numbered capture (named like any capture) and copies it; the original stays byte for byte as it was. The mini-map, zoom at the pointer and panning follow the turned picture.
    • Thumbnails. G (the window grows by the strip's height, so the picture keeps its room) shows a strip of the folder's thumbnails along the bottom with the current picture marked; click one to jump, scroll with the wheel, and it follows you. They are made after the picture is on screen, on a low-priority thread, kept in memory and never written to disk. Off to begin with; A9 View remembers your choice.
    • Info. I opens a panel with the name, folder, type, size on disk, pixel size and modified and created dates, and, when the file carries them, the camera, lens, exposure, date taken and location. Every value can be copied. A location shows as coordinates with Open in maps, which opens a map in your browser: that click is the only way anything about a picture leaves your PC. The panel only reads.
    • Stay on top (T), with a pin in the title bar while it is on. Rename (F2): a box at the top of the window renames the file in place; the ending cannot be changed, and a name that exists or isn't allowed says why and changes nothing. Renaming a capture takes its edit original, its source note, its edited mark and its searchable text with it, and its number stays taken.
    • Order and looping. Sort by name (the default, Explorer's way), date modified or size, ascending or descending, from Settings or the right-click menu; Loop at the ends can be turned off. "12 of 48" in the title follows the order. Transparent pictures can show a checkerboard (default) or the dark ground.
    • Open more. Ctrl+O opens a file dialog; dropping a picture or a folder on the window opens it (a folder opens at its first picture). The title bar also shows the file's size.
    • Settings. The A9 View section now has five groups: Windows, Window, Mouse, Display and Folder. The manual page, Keyboard shortcuts, Settings, Privacy and the README say so.
    • Test scripts fixed. The Delete end-to-end test now waits until the other process really holds the file before it clicks (a fixed pause raced a slow start), and the editor test's strip-menu expectation follows the menu as it has been since 0.13.0 (Share is its second item). The Home and viewer tests follow the new section name and the size in the title. No app behavior changed for these.

    0.14.2 · November 24, 2026#

    • The picker no longer plays Windows' window animation. When you released a selection, Windows faded the full-screen picker out with its own close animation (the one Animation effects in Windows' Accessibility settings controls). The picker shows a frozen picture of your screen, so the fade looked like the whole screen lifting off, and the tail of it was a black sheet on every monitor. The picker windows (and the outline round a recording or scrolling capture) now have that animation switched off for themselves, so they open and close at once whatever your Animation effects setting is. Other windows are unchanged. The log says picker windows: 3 made, window animation turned off on 3 at start. 0.14.1's change (hide the windows first, then clear them) stays: it was correct, but it was not what you saw. This was found from a log and a setting, not from seeing it here: it is not yet confirmed on a PC that showed it.

    0.14.1 · November 24, 2026#

    • The picker is hidden before its frozen picture is cleared. When a capture finished, each screen could flash black for a moment. The picker windows were told to hide, but the hide only took effect once the rest of the release had been handled, and by then each screen's frozen picture had already been cleared, so the window showed its black background. Now every picker window is hidden first (the call returns once it has taken effect), and only then are the pictures cleared, on release, on Esc and for secure, recording, scrolling and Copy text captures alike. Nothing is slower. The log gets one new line per picker, picker closed: how=commit|cancel ... hidden=…ms cleared=…ms, which says how long after the release the windows were hidden and the pictures cleared (numbers only). This was fixed from reading the code, not from seeing the flash, so it is not yet confirmed on a PC that showed it.

    0.14.0 · November 24, 2026#

    • A9 Snap now opens your pictures. Double-click a picture (or choose Open with, then A9 Snap) and the viewer shows it at once, in a window the size of the picture: pictures smaller than your screen open at 100%, never scaled up, and bigger ones are scaled to fit inside 90% of the screen's usable area. If A9 Snap is already running it shows the picture straight away; if not, it starts, shows the picture first and then finishes starting up. It is free.
    • The whole folder is a wheel-click away. The mouse wheel moves to the next or previous picture in name order, the way Explorer sorts names (2 before 10), wrapping at the ends; Ctrl+wheel zooms at the pointer (a setting swaps the two). Left, Right, PgUp, PgDn, Home and End move; Ctrl+0 fits, Ctrl+1 is 100%; drag moves a zoomed picture; F11 or a double-click is full screen; Esc leaves it and then closes; Delete sends the file to the Recycle Bin with an Undo note; Ctrl+C copies the picture and the file; E edits; F1 opens the manual. The next and previous pictures are read ahead, so a step shows at once. The title bar says the name, "12 of 48", the size in pixels and the zoom.
    • PNG, JPG, GIF and WebP (animated ones play), AVIF, BMP, ICO and SVG are shown through the web view (an SVG is only a picture: nothing in it runs); TIFF and HEIC are decoded by Windows itself. A photo's stored rotation is respected. When Windows has no decoder for a type, the window says which one and what Windows needs; a picture that can't be read shows its name and why.
    • Right-click the picture for Edit, Copy, Copy text, Redact and Share (the last three are Pro and show the PRO badge without it), Show in folder and Delete, or drag the picture out into another app.
    • E on a picture that isn't a capture opens the editor on a copy. Saving always writes the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, named like any capture; the original is never changed and no .a9snap folder is made next to it. TIFF and HEIC are turned into PNG for the editor.
    • A9 Snap offers itself and never takes over. The installer (and the Store package, through its manifest) tells Windows that A9 Snap can open these types, so it shows up under Open with and in Default apps. It never changes what opens your pictures today, and uninstalling removes what it added. Click the bare desktop, or press F, for the whole monitor the pointer is on (outlined and labelled Screen so you can see it first). Drag still takes a region, and the outline goes away once the drag passes 4 pixels. A window that spans two monitors is cut to the monitor you click on. Alt+X (OCR capture) uses the same picker.
    • A click no longer cancels. It takes the window under it, or the screen. Esc and right-click still cancel.
    • A key hint at the bottom of the monitor you are on: Click a window · Drag a region · F full screen · Esc cancel. It shows for the first 3 seconds of each picker, and the whole time for your first 5 captures after install.

    0.3.6 · October 27, 2026#

    • A9 Snap is packaged for the Microsoft Store. The release now also builds A9Snap_<version>_x64.msix, the same program in an MSIX package that Microsoft signs after it passes certification, so a Store install has no unknown-publisher warning and Windows does the updating. The installer from a9snap.com and its updater are unchanged.
    • The Store version behaves like a Store app. It makes no update check and shows no update controls (Updates and about says "Updates come from the Microsoft Store."); Start at login uses Windows' startup task, off until you switch it on, and the restart recovery from 0.3.2 works the same way; settings and the log live in the app's own folder (the manual says where), your screenshots stay in your screenshots folder. If the installer copy is installed too, the Store version tells you once that the two don't share settings.

    0.3.5 · October 27, 2026#

    • OCR capture copies text, not a picture. Press Alt+X, drag around text in the same pixel-precise picker, and the recognized words go on the clipboard as plain text. No PNG is saved and the capture numbering does not move. Text copied confirms it; No text found or a recognition problem leaves the clipboard alone. The hotkey is configurable in Shortcuts, with its own conflict warning, and the tray menu has an OCR capture item.
    • Copy text from an existing capture. Right-click a capture in the recent strip and choose Copy text, or use the editor's new Copy text button. The editor recognizes the picture currently on screen, including an unsaved crop or annotation, and stays open; neither path changes the capture or writes another file.
    • Recognition is completely local. A9 Snap uses Windows' built-in OCR and your installed OCR languages. Pictures go to the recognizer in memory only: no upload, no temporary image or text files, and no recognized text in the log.

    0.3.4 · October 27, 2026#

    • Delete a screenshot from Home. Point at a capture in Recent captures and a small red trash can appears in its corner (also when it has the keyboard focus; always visible on a touch screen). Click it, or press Delete on a focused capture: it fades out, the grid fills with the next-newest one, and a note at the bottom says "Deleted snag_0042.png · Undo" for 6 seconds. The file and, if you had edited it, its hidden original in .a9snap go to the Recycle Bin, never away for good, and Undo puts both back under the same names. If Windows can't move the file (another app has it open, say) the capture stays and the note says why.
    • The strip has the same. Delete takes the picked captures (or the focused one) to the Recycle Bin with one "Deleted 3 screenshots · Undo" note, a small trash can shows on a card under the pointer, and the right-click menu's Delete takes the whole selection a card belongs to.
    • A capture deleted from A9 Snap keeps its number while the app is running: deleting the newest one no longer hands its number to the next capture, so "Next file" stays where it was. (Files deleted in Explorer still follow the folder, as before.)

    0.3.3 · October 27, 2026#

    • The A9 Snap manual is at a9snap.com/docs: how to capture, the recent captures strip, the editor (and what Save, Save as new and Revert do to your files), every setting, Start at login, keyboard shortcuts, privacy, and what to do when something goes wrong. It has a search box, screenshots, and an "On this page" list on each page.
    • A Manual row in the Home window (under the section menu) opens it in your browser, and a ? button at the top right of every section opens the page for that section. F1 with the Home window in front does the same, and the tray menu has a Manual item above Settings.

    0.3.2 · October 27, 2026#

    • A9 Snap comes back after Windows closes it. When Windows closes every app for a Restart that never finishes (or is cancelled), no sign-in follows, so the login entry never fires and A9 Snap stayed gone. Now, when Windows ends the session, A9 Snap sets a one-time task in your own Task Scheduler to start it again two minutes later. If the PC really restarts, the login entry starts it and removes the task; every start removes it. A9 Snap never holds up a shutdown, and asks Windows to restart it after a crash.
    • The switch shows what Windows will really do, not just what was saved. If you turned A9 Snap off in Windows' Startup apps, Shortcuts keeps the switch on and says "Windows has turned this off in Startup apps." with a Fix in Windows button that opens that page. A9 Snap never switches Windows' own setting back on for you.
    • The login entry is checked on every launch. It is written with the program's path in quotes (the install folder has a space in it) and, if it is missing, unquoted, points at another copy (a dev build, an old path) or is out of date after an update, A9 Snap puts it right; if you switch Start at login off, it is removed.
    • The log now says how A9 Snap ended: quit from the tray, Windows ending the session (and whether the session is really ending), a crash, or a normal exit.
    • A launch at login goes straight to the tray: no window, no confirmation, the hotkeys ready. New installs have Start at login on; if you had turned it off, it stays off.

    0.3.1 · October 24, 2026#

    • A new Home window replaces the single scrolling Settings page. Double-click the tray icon (or pick Settings in the tray menu) and it opens on Home: a side column with Capture, Recent captures, Editor (opens your newest capture) and Folder buttons, each showing your own hotkey, and a menu of sections: Home, Shortcuts, Saving, Strip and editor, Notifications, Updates and about.
    • Home shows your six newest captures as thumbnails, with the file name, how long ago, and the Volt dot on edited ones; click one to open it in the editor. Under them: where the next file goes (and what it will be called) and what happens after a capture.
    • Every setting is still there, in the section it belongs to, with the values you already had. The window is 1000 × 680 (it can be made as small as 860 × 600, and then the right-hand side scrolls); the version and an update card with Update now stay in the side column.

    0.3.0 · October 23, 2026#

    • The editor: draw on a capture and send it. Open it from the recent captures strip (E, a double-click, or right-click → Edit), from the Edit button on the "Copied · …" confirmation, or after every capture (Settings, "Open the editor after every capture", off by default).
    • Tools, each with a key: Arrow (A), Box (B), Ellipse (E), Highlight (H), Text (T), Blur (U), Crop (C), and Select (V). Five colours (Nine Red, Volt, Ink, white, blue) and thin, medium or thick. Every shape stays editable until you save: select it, move it, drag its handles, change its colour, delete it. Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) go back through the whole session.
    • Blur pixelates the region for real: the saved file holds flat blocks, not the picture with a layer on top. Text is Geist with a soft dark outline so it reads on any background. Crop: drag a region, Enter applies, undo brings the whole picture back. Ctrl+wheel zooms, Ctrl+0 is 100 %.
    • Ctrl+S or Done saves over the same file, so the numbering never changes, and puts the result on the clipboard as the picture and as the file ("Saved · … · copied"). Ctrl+Shift+S saves as the next numbered file instead. Esc with unsaved changes asks in the window: Save, Discard or Cancel.
    • The untouched original is kept in a hidden .a9snap folder inside your screenshots folder, so Revert can put it back. The numbering ignores that folder. A capture that was edited shows a small Volt dot in the strip. The original still holds whatever a blur hid, so delete the .a9snap folder before you share the whole screenshots folder.
    • The confirmation toast now stays 3.5 seconds when it has an Edit button, and is a little wider.

    0.2.1 · October 23, 2026#

    • Recent captures: a Volt Copy to clipboard button across the top of the strip, so you can pick with the mouse and copy without reaching for Enter. It says what one click copies ("Copy to clipboard", "Copy 3 to clipboard") and is off, reading "Select captures to copy", until something is selected. It does what Enter does (one capture as the picture plus its file, several as files, oldest first), says "Copied 3 captures" and closes the strip. Enter and Ctrl+C still work, and the button never takes the keyboard from the cards.
    • Double-click the tray icon to open Settings (or bring it forward). Right-click still shows the menu; a single left click still shows it too, a moment later, after the double-click time has passed.

    0.2.0 · October 23, 2026#

    • Recent captures: press Alt+Shift+Z (or pick it in the tray menu) for a strip of your newest captures, newest first, from the screenshots folder (6, 9 or 12 of them: Settings, "Show in strip"). It opens at once with placeholders and the thumbnails fill in; they are kept in memory only, never written to disk.
    • Select with a click or the keys 1 to 9 (Ctrl+A for all, arrows and Space to walk and toggle), then Enter or Ctrl+C: one capture goes to the clipboard as the picture plus its file, several go as files, oldest first, so they paste in the order you took them. A toast says "Copied 4 captures".
    • Drag one capture, or the whole selection, out of the strip as files into Explorer, a browser chat box, Teams or Outlook. Right-click a card: Open, Show in folder, Copy path, Delete (to the Recycle Bin, with an Undo on the toast).
    • Esc, a click outside, or the hotkey again closes the strip without touching the clipboard.
    • Clicking the "Copied · …" toast after a capture opens the strip with that capture selected (Shift+click still shows the file in its folder).
    • Settings: a "Recent captures hotkey" row beside the capture hotkey, with the same warning in Settings and the tray when another app owns the key.

    0.1.1 · October 23, 2026#

    • Numbering continues an existing folder: the scan ignores case and treats -, _, a space or nothing as the same separator, and the next file copies the style of the highest-numbered one (SNAG-7094.png is followed by SNAG-7095.png, whatever prefix Settings says). The count is read from the folder on every capture; gaps are never filled and a file is never overwritten.
    • Settings shows the real next file name, read from the folder and refreshed as you change the folder or the prefix (it used to show a fixed example).
    • The crosshair, bullseye and loupe are drawn only on the monitor the pointer is on, also while you drag from one monitor to another.
    • The approved A9 Snap look: Ink and Graphite surfaces, Volt accent, the Bullseye icon (app, tray and installer) and the A9 Snap lockup in the Settings header, Geist and Geist Mono.
    • Dev releases are marked as pre-releases, so they never take GitHub's Latest badge.
