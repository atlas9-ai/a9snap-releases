    The picture viewer

    Double-click any picture and A9 Snap shows it at once, in a window the size of the picture, with the rest of the folder one wheel-click away. Then edit it, copy it or send it. It is free, it writes nothing about what you look at, and it never makes itself your default app.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.14.0

    The viewer is for pictures that did not come from A9 Snap: a photo from your phone, a download, a logo, a diagram. It opens quickly, shows the picture at its own size, and puts the same tools you use on captures one key away. It is part of the free app.

    Open a picture#

    First choose A9 Snap in Windows (see Choose A9 Snap in Windows below), then double-click a picture in Explorer. You can also right-click a picture and choose Open with, then A9 Snap, without choosing anything first.

    • If A9 Snap is already running, it shows the picture in the viewer window at once. If it is not, it starts, shows the picture first and then finishes starting up, and stays in the tray as after any start.
    • Select several pictures and press Enter: the viewer shows the first one.
    • One viewer window is used for everything. Opening another picture replaces the one on screen. Closing the window hides it; it is kept ready for the next one.

    A9 Snap opens PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, ICO, SVG, TIFF and HEIC pictures. An animated GIF or WebP plays. An SVG is shown as a picture only: nothing in it runs. A photo's stored rotation is respected, so a phone picture is upright. TIFF and HEIC are read by Windows itself; if your PC has no decoder for a type, the window says which type it cannot open and what Windows needs (for HEIC, the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store), and the wheel still moves to the next picture. A picture that cannot be read (damaged, locked or gone) shows its name and the reason in the window.

    Size and place#

    By default the window fits the picture. A picture smaller than your screen opens at 100%, never scaled up: one picture pixel is one screen pixel, so it is sharp at any display scale. A bigger one is scaled down to fit inside 90% of the screen's usable area. The window opens in the middle of the monitor your pointer is on, and never smaller than its title bar needs.

    Change this with Viewer window in Settings: Fit each picture (the default) or Keep my size and place, where the window stays where and how big you left it and each picture fits inside it.

    Moving and zooming#

    Do thisWhat happens
    Mouse wheelThe next or previous picture in the same folder.
    Ctrl+wheelZooms in and out around the pointer.
    Left / Right, PgUp / PgDnPrevious and next picture.
    Home / EndThe first and the last picture.
    Ctrl+0Fits the picture to the window.
    Ctrl+1Shows it at 100%.
    DragMoves a zoomed picture around.
    Double-click or F11Full screen on and off.
    EscLeaves full screen, then closes the window.
    DeleteSends the file to the Recycle Bin. A note offers Undo (also Ctrl+Z). It is never deleted for good.
    Ctrl+CCopies the picture and the file, as a capture is copied.
    EEdits the picture. See below.
    F1Opens this page.

    Mouse wheel in the viewer in Settings swaps the wheel and Ctrl+wheel, if you would rather zoom with the wheel alone.

    Pictures come in name order, the way Explorer sorts names: 2 comes before 10. After the last picture the wheel wraps to the first. Only picture types count; videos are skipped. The next and previous pictures are read ahead, so a step shows at once.

    The title bar shows the file name, where it is in the folder ("12 of 48"), its size in pixels and the zoom. Nothing else is on screen until you move the pointer: then arrows appear at the sides.

    The right-click menu#

    Right-click the picture for Edit, Copy, Copy text, Redact, Share, Show in folder and Delete. Copy text, Redact and Share are part of A9 Snap Pro: without it they carry a small PRO badge and open the Pro sheet. Redact copies the picture with its email addresses, phone numbers and card numbers blurred; the file is not changed. See Auto-redact.

    You can also drag the picture out of the window and drop the file into another app, as in the strip. While you are zoomed in, a drag moves the picture instead; hold Alt to carry the file out.

    Edit a picture that is not a capture#

    Press E on a capture (a numbered file in your screenshots folder) and the editor opens on it, as it does everywhere else.

    Press E on any other picture and the editor opens on a copy. Saving always writes the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, named like any capture, and copies it. There is no "save over the same file": your original is never changed, and no .a9snap folder is made next to it. TIFF and HEIC pictures are turned into PNG for the editor.

    Choose A9 Snap in Windows#

    Windows lets only you decide which app opens your pictures, so A9 Snap never makes itself the default. What it does is tell Windows that it can open these types, so it appears under Open with and in Windows' Default apps list. Installing A9 Snap changes nothing about what opens your pictures today, and uninstalling removes what it added.

    In Settings, the Viewer section has Choose A9 Snap in Windows. It opens Windows' Default apps page for A9 Snap, where you pick the picture types you want it to open, and it lists the types that open with A9 Snap now. On the Microsoft Store version A9 Snap offers the same types through its package.

    Privacy#

    The viewer reads the picture and the names of the other files in its folder, and writes nothing: no thumbnails, no list of what you viewed, no index. The log says the type, the sizes and the times, never a file or folder name. Secure captures and the secure vault are never opened by the viewer. The viewer makes no network call.
