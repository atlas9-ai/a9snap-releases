    Scrolling capture

    Capture a whole web page, chat or long list as one tall picture. A9 Snap scrolls it for you and stitches what it sees. It is a Pro feature.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.12.0

    A long page doesn't fit on one screen. Scrolling capture scrolls the area you choose by itself, takes a picture after each scroll, and joins the pictures into one tall PNG that goes through the usual path: onto the clipboard, into your screenshots folder with the next number, into the strip, with the toast and its Edit button. It is part of A9 Snap Pro: see Pro features.

    Take one#

    1. Press Alt+Z.
    2. Press L (for long). The size says Scroll, for example Scroll · 1280 × 720. (You can also press L after you start dragging, or just before you let go.)
    3. Click a window, or drag a region inside the part that scrolls. Dragging only the scrolling area, without the window's title bar and toolbars, gives the cleanest picture.

    Scrolling starts the moment you let go. If you changed the capture hotkey, the keys follow it: your hotkey, then L. The picker's hint line says L scroll (Pro). L is never sticky: the next capture starts as a normal picture. L goes with S (secure capture): a secure scrolling capture is a secure picture. It replaces R (record video): press one and the other turns off.

    Without Pro, L does not scroll. The picker says Scrolling capture is part of A9 Snap Pro, the capture is a normal picture, and the Pro sheet opens once after the picker closes.

    While it scrolls#

    A small bar sits just outside the area, with a Volt dot, the height of the picture so far, and Stop. A thin Volt outline marks the area. Neither is ever in the picture.

    A9 Snap turns the mouse wheel over the middle of the area (it moves the pointer there while it works and puts it back afterwards), waits for the page to stop moving, takes a frame, and lines it up with the one before by finding the rows they share. It adapts how far it scrolls to how far your app moves for each notch, so frames overlap by about half.

    It stops by itself when:

    • the page ends (nothing moves any more), or
    • the picture reaches its height limit (see Settings below), or
    • you stop it: press Stop, Esc, or the capture hotkey again. A9 Snap keeps everything captured so far.

    Headers and footers that stay put while the page scrolls (a sticky menu bar, a status line) are kept once, at the top and bottom of the picture, not repeated in every frame. A scroll bar at the right edge is ignored when frames are lined up.

    A page that is shorter than the area gives an ordinary picture, and the toast says nothing scrolled.

    If it can't line the frames up#

    If two scrolls in a row can't be lined up (the page jumps, the content changes while it scrolls), the bar says Scroll it yourself, then press Stop. Scroll with the mouse or keyboard, a screen at a time or less, and A9 Snap keeps stitching what it sees until you press Stop.

    What you get#

    • One PNG, the next number in your screenshots folder, such as snag_0043.png, on the clipboard as a picture. Also copy as file works as for any capture.
    • A toast that says Copied · snag_0043.png, with Edit. If the height limit cut the picture, it says stopped at 20,000 px.
    • The app and window it came from are noted like any picture's, and you can search for them in the strip.
    • A secure scrolling capture goes where a secure capture goes.

    Settings#

    Open Saving in the Home window.

    • Tallest scrolling capture. 10,000, 20,000 (the default) or 40,000 pixels. A very tall picture on a wide screen takes a lot of memory and a few seconds to save.

    Works best with#

    Scrolling capture works best in web browsers and most lists and documents: anything that scrolls smoothly when you turn the mouse wheel over it. Some apps scroll in ways that can't be stitched: pages that move or animate as they scroll, content that loads or changes on its own, apps that ignore the mouse wheel, and views that redraw differently at every position. You get the manual fallback or a partial picture there.

    It captures the picture a screen at a time, so it can only capture what the app can scroll to. It does not scroll sideways, and PDF viewers are not supported.

    Privacy#

    Scrolling capture happens on your PC and the pictures stay there. It only looks at the area you chose and puts the finished picture where any capture goes. Nothing is uploaded and the log says only how many frames there were and how big the picture is. See Privacy.
