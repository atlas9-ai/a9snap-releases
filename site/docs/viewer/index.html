    A9 View

    Double-click any picture or video and A9 Snap shows it at once, in a window the size of the picture, with the rest of the folder one wheel-click away. Zoom with a small map of where you are, turn a picture, play a video and save a frame of it, see the folder as thumbnails, read its info, rename it, then edit it, copy it or send it. It is free, it writes nothing about what you look at, and it never makes itself your default app.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.15.0

    A9 View is the part of A9 Snap that looks at pictures and plays videos. It is for files that did not come from A9 Snap too: a photo from your phone, a download, a logo, a diagram, a screen recording. It opens quickly, shows the picture at its own size, and puts the same tools you use on captures one key away. It is part of the free app, and it lives inside it: one install, one icon in the tray. Videos are described in Videos below.

    Open a picture#

    First choose A9 Snap in Windows (see Choose A9 Snap in Windows below), then double-click a picture or a video in Explorer. You can also right-click one and choose Open with, then A9 Snap, without choosing anything first.

    • If A9 Snap is already running, it shows the picture in the A9 View window at once. If it is not, it starts, shows the picture first and then finishes starting up, and stays in the tray as after any start.
    • Select several pictures and press Enter: A9 View shows the first one.
    • Inside A9 View, press Ctrl+O (or the Open button) for a file dialog, or drop a picture, a video or a folder on the window. A folder opens at its first item.
    • One window is used for everything. Opening another picture replaces the one on screen. Closing the window hides it; it is kept ready for the next one.

    A9 Snap opens PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, AVIF, BMP, ICO, SVG, TIFF and HEIC pictures. An animated GIF or WebP plays. An SVG is shown as a picture only: nothing in it runs. A photo's stored rotation is respected, so a phone picture is upright. TIFF and HEIC are read by Windows itself; if your PC has no decoder for a type, the window says which type it cannot open and what Windows needs (for HEIC, the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store), and the wheel still moves to the next picture. A picture that cannot be read (damaged, locked or gone) shows its name and the reason in the window.

    Size and place#

    By default the window fits the picture. A picture smaller than your screen opens at 100%, never scaled up: one picture pixel is one screen pixel, so it is sharp at any display scale. A bigger one is scaled down to fit inside 90% of the screen's usable area. The window opens in the middle of the monitor your pointer is on, and never smaller than its title bar needs.

    Change this with A9 View window in Settings: Fit each picture (the default) or Keep my size and place, where the window stays where and how big you left it and each picture fits inside it.

    Mouse wheel in A9 View in Settings swaps the wheel and Ctrl+wheel, if you would rather zoom with the wheel alone.

    The title bar shows the file name, where it is in the folder ("12 of 48"), its size in pixels and on disk, and the zoom. Nothing else is on screen until you move the pointer: then the toolbar and arrows appear.

    The mini-map#

    When the picture is bigger than the window shows, a small overview of the whole picture appears at the bottom right, with a rectangle for the part you are looking at. Click or drag in it to move around. The cross closes it until your next zoom, and it hides by itself when the whole picture is in view. It never covers the toolbar. Mini-map in Settings turns it off for good.

    The toolbar#

    Move the pointer over the window and a bar appears at the bottom, then fades after a moment. It holds Open, Zoom out, Zoom in, Fit or 100%, Previous, Next, Rotate left, Rotate right, Flip, Stay on top, Info, Thumbnails and A9 View settings (which opens Settings on this section). Every button's tooltip names its key. Right-click Previous or Next to jump to the first or last picture, or back or forward 10.

    Toolbar in Settings keeps the bar always shown, or never shown.

    Videos#

    A9 View plays MP4, M4V, MOV and WebM videos in the same window, with the same wheel, arrows, zoom, full screen, thumbnails, info, rename and Delete. An animated GIF stays a picture. Nothing here changes or overwrites a video file.

    • Opening. Double-click a video in Explorer (once you have chosen A9 Snap for that type), drop one on the window, press Ctrl+O, or start A9 Snap with its path. A video opened this way plays at once. A video you arrive at by moving through the folder shows its first frame and a play button and waits, unless Play videos as I move through a folder is on in Settings.
    • A9 Snap's own recordings open here too: from the Home window, the strip and the note after a recording. Open my recordings in A9 View in Settings (on by default) switches it; off, they open in the app Windows uses for them.
    • The window opens at the video's own pixel size under the same rule as pictures (Fit each picture or Keep my size and place). Ctrl+wheel zoom, dragging and the mini-map work on a video as on a picture. Rotate and flip do nothing on a video.
    • A video is read from disk as it plays, a piece at a time, never loaded whole: a two-hour or multi-gigabyte recording opens as fast as a short one.

    Controls#

    A row of controls sits just above the toolbar and shows and hides with it: play and pause, a scrub bar with the time so far and the length (drag it and the picture follows), volume and mute, speed (0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 times) and loop.

    Do thisWhat happens
    Click the video, or SpacePlays and pauses. At the end it stops on the last frame, unless loop is on.
    Shift+Left / Shift+RightJumps 5 seconds back or forward.
    , / .Steps one frame back or forward (the video pauses first).
    MMutes and unmutes.
    SSave this frame (below).
    Left / Right and the wheelStill the previous and next item in the folder, so you are never stuck on a video.

    The volume and the mute switch are remembered between sessions. The speed is 1 each time A9 Snap starts.

    Save this frame#

    S, the camera button on the toolbar, or Save this frame in the right-click menu saves the frame on screen as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder (named like any capture) and copies it. It is the whole video frame at the video's own pixel size, not a picture of the window. It is free.

    What a video shows#

    The title bar adds the video's length ("clip.mp4 · 3 of 9 · 800 × 450 · 0:05 · 1.2 MB · 100%"). In the thumbnail strip a video shows a frame with a small play mark, made in memory like the rest and never saved. The info panel (I) adds the length, frame rate, video format and audio format, and whether the video has sound. The right-click menu has Save this frame, Copy (the file), Share, Show in folder and Delete; the text and picture tools are for pictures.

    A video A9 View can't play#

    A file with a video ending that is not a video, or a format the built-in player does not know, shows A9 View can't play this video with Open with another app, which asks Windows for its Open with list for that file. The wheel and the arrows still move on to the other items. A9 View does not promise to play every format.

    Stopping#

    Playback stops, and the file is released, when you move to another item, close the window or A9 View hides. After closing, no sound goes on and you can rename, move or delete the file at once. Delete and F2 work on a video as on a picture.

    Rotate and flip#

    R turns the picture a quarter turn to the right and Shift+R to the left. H flips it left to right and V top to bottom. This is only a view: the file is never changed, and the picture goes back to normal when you move to another one. The mini-map, zooming at the pointer and dragging follow the turned picture.

    To keep the turned picture, right-click and choose Save a copy. It is written as the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, named like any capture, and copied. The original stays exactly as it was. Dragging the picture out into another app, and Copy, give the file as it is on disk (unless you saved a copy).

    Thumbnails#

    G (or the Thumbnails button) shows a row of thumbnails of the folder along the bottom, with the current picture marked. Click one to jump to it; the wheel over the strip scrolls it, and it follows you as you move. The thumbnails are made after the picture is on screen, held in memory only and never saved anywhere. The strip is off to begin with, and A9 View remembers if you turn it on.

    Info#

    I opens a panel at the side with the picture's name, folder, type, size on disk, pixel size, and modified and created dates. When the file carries them it also shows the camera, lens, exposure, date taken and location. A value can be copied with the button beside it. The panel only reads; it edits nothing.

    A location shows as coordinates with Open in maps, which opens a map in your browser. That click is the only way anything about a picture leaves your PC: A9 Snap builds the link from the picture's own coordinates, and sends nothing until you click. See Privacy.

    Stay on top#

    T keeps the A9 View window above your other windows until you turn it off or close the window. A pin shows in the title bar while it is on.

    Rename#

    F2 renames the picture in its folder. A box opens at the top of the window with the name (without its ending); Enter renames and Esc cancels. The ending stays as it is: renaming never converts a picture. A name that is already taken, or that Windows does not allow, says why and changes nothing. Renaming a capture takes its edit original, its note of where it came from and its searchable text with it, and its number stays taken. Because a capture is found by its numbered name, a capture renamed to something else no longer shows in the strip or on Home.

    Order and looping#

    Items come in name order, the way Explorer sorts names: 2 comes before 10. Right-click the picture, or open Settings, to sort by name, date modified or size, ascending or descending. Loop at the ends (on by default) makes the next item after the last the first, and the other way round; turn it off and the wheel stops at the ends. Pictures and videos are items of the same folder and share one count and one order; Include videos when moving through a folder in Settings leaves the videos out. The next and previous pictures are read ahead, so a step shows at once.

    Transparent pictures#

    Transparent pictures in Settings chooses what shows behind the see-through parts of a picture: a checkerboard (the default) or the dark ground of the window.

    The right-click menu#

    Right-click the picture for Edit, Copy, Copy text, Redact, Share, Show in folder and Delete, then Save a copy while the picture is turned, and the folder's order and Loop at the ends. On a video the menu is Save this frame, Copy, Share, Show in folder and Delete, then the same order and Loop at the ends. Copy text, Redact and Share are part of A9 Snap Pro: without it they carry a small PRO badge and open the Pro sheet. Redact copies the picture with its email addresses, phone numbers and card numbers blurred; the file is not changed. See Auto-redact.

    You can also drag the picture out of the window and drop the file into another app, as in the strip. While you are zoomed in, a drag moves the picture instead; hold Alt to carry the file out.

    Edit a picture that is not a capture#

    Press E on a capture (a numbered file in your screenshots folder) and the editor opens on it, as it does everywhere else.

    Press E on any other picture and the editor opens on a copy. Saving always writes the next numbered capture in your screenshots folder, named like any capture, and copies it. There is no "save over the same file": your original is never changed, and no .a9snap folder is made next to it. TIFF and HEIC pictures are turned into PNG for the editor.

    Choose A9 Snap in Windows#

    Windows lets only you decide which app opens your pictures and videos, so A9 Snap never makes itself the default. What it does is tell Windows that it can open these types, so it appears under Open with and in Windows' Default apps list. Installing A9 Snap changes nothing about what opens your pictures or videos today, and uninstalling removes what it added.

    In Settings, the A9 View section has Choose A9 Snap in Windows. It opens Windows' Default apps page for A9 Snap, where you pick the types you want it to open, and it lists the types that open with A9 Snap now: the picture types on one line and the video types on their own line, so nobody gets video by surprise. On the Microsoft Store version A9 Snap offers the same types through its package.

    Privacy#

    A9 View reads the picture or video and the names of the other files in its folder, and writes nothing: no thumbnails on disk, no frames, no list of what you viewed, no index. The only file it can write is one you ask for (Save a copy, Save this frame, or saving in the editor). A video is read through A9 Snap's own local route, a piece at a time, and no network is involved; nothing is held open between pieces, so the file is free the moment it stops playing. The log says the type, the sizes and the times, never a file or folder name. Secure captures and the secure vault are never opened by A9 View. It makes no network call; the one thing that leaves your PC is the map link you open yourself from the info panel.
