    Getting started

    Install A9 Snap, take your first screenshot, and find your way around the tray icon and the Home window.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.5.2

    A9 Snap is a free screenshot tool for Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit). You press a key, then click a window, press F for the whole screen, or drag a box around part of it. The picture is on your clipboard, ready to paste, and saved as a numbered PNG file. There is no account to make and nothing to sign in to.

    Install#

    The easiest way to get A9 Snap is the Microsoft Store. It installs with no Windows warning, and Windows keeps it up to date for you.

    1. Click the badge above. A9 Snap's page in the Microsoft Store opens in a new tab.
    2. Click Get (or Install). Windows installs A9 Snap for you.
    3. Open A9 Snap once after installing, from the Store page or the Start menu. The Store doesn't start it for you, and that first launch is when A9 Snap switches on Start at login. It opens the Home window so you can see it is running.

    If A9 Snap does not open, find A9 Snap in the Start menu.

    Already installed A9 Snap from a9snap.com? Keep using it. The Store version is a separate install with its own settings.

    If your work PC blocks the Microsoft Store

    Some work PCs do not allow the Microsoft Store. Install A9 Snap with the installer from a9snap.com instead.

    1. On a9snap.com, click Download the installer, under the Store badge. Your browser saves A9Snap-Setup-x64.exe.
    2. Open the file. Early builds are not signed with a Windows certificate yet, so Windows may show Windows protected your PC first. Click More info, then Run anyway. The Microsoft Store version avoids this step.
    3. Follow the installer. It installs for your Windows user only and does not ask for administrator rights.
    4. A9 Snap starts by itself when the installer finishes. The first time, it opens the Home window so you can see it is running.

    Take your first screenshot#

    1. Press Alt+Z from any app. The screen freezes and dims, and a crosshair follows your pointer.
    2. Click a window to capture it (it is outlined as you point), press F for the whole screen, or click and drag a box around what you want. A small readout shows its size, such as 640 × 220.
    3. Let go. The picture is now on your clipboard and saved in your screenshots folder. A small note at the bottom right says Copied and gives the file name.
    4. Switch to a chat, an email or a document and press Ctrl+V.

    To change your mind, press Esc before you let go. Take a screenshot covers windows, the whole screen, the crosshair, the magnifier and several monitors. To copy the words rather than a picture, press Alt+X instead and select the text: local OCR puts plain text on the clipboard and saves no file.

    The tray icon#

    A9 Snap waits in the tray, the row of small icons next to the clock. Its icon is the Bullseye. If you do not see it, click the ^ arrow by the clock to show hidden icons. Closing the A9 Snap window does not quit it, so the hotkeys keep working.

    • Double-click the icon to open the Home window.
    • Right-click the icon for the menu: Capture region, OCR capture, Recent captures, Open screenshots folder, Manual, Settings, Check for updates and Quit. A single left click shows the same menu a moment later.
    • A small warning badge on the icon means another app has taken one of your hotkeys. See If something goes wrong.

    To quit A9 Snap, right-click the icon and choose Quit.

    The Home window#

    Home is where you start. On the left are the Capture and Recent captures buttons, each showing its hotkey, then Editor and Folder, and a menu of settings sections. In the middle are your six newest captures. Click one to draw on it. The Home window explains every part. The Manual row under the menu opens this manual in your browser.
