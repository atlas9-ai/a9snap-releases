    The recent captures strip

    One key shows your newest captures as thumbnails. Pick some and copy them, drag them into a chat, or open one to draw on it.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.5.2

    The strip is for the moment after you have taken a few screenshots and want to send some of them. It shows your newest captures, newest first, taken from the screenshots folder.

    Open the strip#

    Do any one of these:

    • Press Alt+Shift+Z.
    • Choose Recent captures from the tray menu.
    • Click Recent captures or Open the strip on the Home window.
    • Click the note that appears after a capture. The strip opens with that capture already selected.

    The strip appears near the bottom of the monitor your pointer is on. It opens at once with placeholders, and the thumbnails fill in a moment later. Thumbnails are kept in memory only and never written to disk.

    To change the hotkey, see Shortcuts.

    Pick captures and copy them#

    1. Select a capture. Click it, or press its number, 1 to 9. Click again to unselect. You can pick as many as you like.
    2. Press Enter or Ctrl+C, or click the Volt Copy to clipboard button at the top. The button says what a click will copy, such as Copy 3 to clipboard, and reads Select captures to copy until you pick something.
    3. Paste with Ctrl+V where you want them.

    What lands on the clipboard depends on how many you picked:

    • One capture goes as the picture plus its file, so it pastes into an image box or an attachment box.
    • Several captures go as files, oldest first, so they paste in the order you took them.
    • Nothing selected: Enter copies the capture that has the focus ring.

    A note says Copied 4 captures and the strip closes.

    To move around with the keyboard, use the arrow keys, Home and End. Space picks or unpicks the focused capture, and Ctrl+A picks all of them.

    Search your captures#

    The strip shows your 12 newest, but the search looks through every capture in the folder.

    • Click the Search captures box at the top, or press Ctrl+F or /. The box does not take the keyboard when the strip opens, so E, Enter, Delete and the arrow keys keep working on the cards until you ask for it.
    • Type part of a file name, such as 07 or snag_01, or the app or the window title the picture came from, such as chrome or quarterly budget. It ignores capital letters. The strip lists every capture that matches, newest first, and says how many (48 captures). See Search by app and window title.
    • The chips under the box narrow the list further: Today, This week (today and the six days before, by the time each file was last saved), Edited (captures you marked up in the editor, the ones with the Volt dot) and up to four app chips. Click a chip to turn it on, and again to turn it off. A chip that is on is Volt. Chips and text work together: both must match.
    • Down, Tab or Enter in the box moves to the first result. Esc in the box clears the text first; press Esc again to close the strip.
    • When nothing matches, the strip says No captures match with a Clear link that turns the search off.

    Picking, copying, dragging, editing and deleting work the same on the results. The search starts empty each time you open the strip.

    Search by app and window title#

    Every file is named snag_0042.png, so A9 Snap also notes which app and window each new capture came from and lets you search by that.

    • A click on a window notes that window. A dragged region notes the topmost window under the middle of the region. The whole screen (F, or a click on the desktop) notes the window you were working in.
    • The app is its friendly name, such as Google Chrome or Outlook; the title is the window's title.
    • After Today, This week and Edited, up to four app chips show the apps you capture most. They say Chrome and Outlook, not Google Chrome and Microsoft Outlook. They work like the other chips and are hidden until at least one capture has an app.
    • Point at a capture to see App · title in its tooltip.
    • Captures from before 0.5.2 have no app. They are still found by file name, date and Edited.

    The notes are kept in .a9snap\captures.json, the hidden folder inside your screenshots folder, next to the originals of edited captures. Window titles can contain an email subject or a document's name. They stay on this PC, are never sent anywhere and never go into the log. To stop noting them, turn off Remember which app each capture came from in Saving; the Clear button beside it deletes what is already noted. See Privacy.

    Scroll to the rest#

    The strip keeps its rows the same height and scrolls. With a search or a chip on, every match is reachable: use the mouse wheel, the arrow keys past the last row, or PageUp and PageDown. The line under the chips says 48 captures when you can scroll to all of them, and Showing 12 of 48 when the strip is only showing your newest. Thumbnails load as the captures scroll into view.

    Edit a capture#

    Press E to open the picked capture in the editor. If you picked none or several, E opens the one with the focus ring. Double-clicking a capture does the same. A small Volt dot next to a name means the capture has been edited and its original is kept.

    The right-click menu#

    Right-click a capture for:

    • Edit opens it in the editor.
    • Copy text recognizes the text in the picture with Windows' built-in OCR and puts it on the clipboard as plain text. It changes no file. A9 Snap says Text copied, or No text found if there was nothing to read.
    • Open opens the file in your default picture app.
    • Show in folder opens Explorer with the file selected.
    • Copy path puts the file's full path on the clipboard.
    • Delete moves the file to the Recycle Bin. If the capture is part of a larger selection, the whole selection goes.

    Delete a screenshot#

    Three ways, all the same:

    • Point at a capture and click the small red trash can that appears in its top-right corner.
    • Pick one or more captures and press Delete. With nothing picked, Delete removes the capture with the focus ring.
    • Choose Delete in the right-click menu.

    The captures go to the Recycle Bin, not away for good, and so does the hidden original the editor kept for an edited one. The strip stays open and fills the space with the next capture. A note says Deleted snag_0042.png, or Deleted 3 screenshots, with an Undo button for 6 seconds. Undo puts the files back under the same names, exactly as they were. After that you can still restore them from the Recycle Bin.

    A deleted capture keeps its number while A9 Snap is running, so the next capture is never given the number of the one you just threw away. If Windows can't move a file, because another app has it open, the capture stays and the note says why.

    Drag captures out#

    Drag one capture, or the whole selection, out of the strip. They go as files, so you can drop them into Explorer, a browser chat box, Teams or Outlook. If the capture you drag is part of a larger selection, the whole selection goes.

    Close the strip#

    Press Esc (twice if the search box has text in it), click anywhere outside it, or press the hotkey again. Closing never touches your clipboard.

    How many captures it shows, and how many to a row#

    The strip shows 12 captures by default, and the search reaches the rest. Captures in the strip in Strip and editor can be 6, 9, 12, 18 or 24, and Per row can be 4, 6, 8 or 12. The default is 12 captures, 6 to a row: two rows of six. The strip is centred on the monitor your pointer is on and never wider than its work area: if a row doesn't fit, it holds fewer, and if the rows don't fit the height, you scroll. The strip lists only files named like your prefix, such as snag_0042.png. See Where screenshots go.
