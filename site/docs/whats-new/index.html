    What's new

    What changed in each version of A9 Snap, newest first.

    Last updated for A9 Snap 0.7.0

    0.7.0 · October 30, 2026#

    • Secure capture, the first Pro feature. Press Alt+Z, then S in the picker: the outline gets a padlock and the size says Secure. Click a window, press F or drag a region as usual. S again turns it off, and it is off at the start of every capture. The picture goes to the clipboard and to a folder of its own (%LOCALAPPDATA%\A9 Snap\Secure by default, numbered there only), or to the clipboard alone with Clipboard only. The toast has a padlock and says Secure · copied (or Secure · copied · saved), with no thumbnail and no Edit button. Without Pro, S shows Secure capture is part of A9 Snap Pro, the capture is a normal one, and the Pro sheet opens once after the picker closes.
    • It stays out of everything else. A secure capture is never in the strip, the Home window, search or the app chips, never in captures.json and never moves the screenshots folder's numbering or Next file. No app or window is noted, the editor never opens for it, and the log says "secure capture saved" with the size and timing, never a file name. The clipboard copy carries the formats that keep it out of Windows' clipboard history and the cloud clipboard, and the secure folder is hidden and marked not to be content-indexed, so Windows Search and Explorer's thumbnail cache leave it alone.
    • A new Secure screenshots card in Saving (with a Pro badge; without Pro it opens the Pro sheet). Secure folder or Clipboard only; the folder with Browse…, Open (on the Microsoft Store version it opens the real folder, not the one Windows redirects AppData into) and Empty now, which asks first, and a warning when the folder is under OneDrive or another synced folder. Clear the secure folder: Never, When A9 Snap quits, After 1 hour, After 24 hours or After 7 days; a picture that came due while the app was closed goes at the next start. Secure pictures are deleted for good, not to the Recycle Bin, and only in a folder A9 Snap made itself. Clear the clipboard after: Off, 30 seconds, 1 minute or 5 minutes, only if the clipboard still holds the secure picture.
    • A new manual page, Secure capture, and the privacy page lists where secure captures go.

    0.6.0 · October 30, 2026#

    • A9 Snap Pro: the foundation. A new Pro section in the Home window says whether this PC is Free or has Pro, and has Get Pro, $14.99, Enter key and Restore purchase, what Pro includes (Secure capture now; Search inside screenshots, Video capture and Scrolling capture coming) and the 14-day money-back guarantee. Pro is a one-time payment for up to 3 of your PCs with every update included. Get Pro opens the checkout in your browser and Pro turns on here, with no restart, as soon as the payment goes through; Enter key unlocks the same way with the key from the purchase email; a key already on three PCs shows the license server's message as it is, and Deactivate this PC frees a seat. A refunded key turns Pro off at the next check. There is no trial, and nothing you have today changes: Free needs no account and no license.
    • A license check, for Pro only. Free never makes one. Once a key is on the PC, A9 Snap asks the license server (a9voice.com) whether it is still good when it starts and once a day, never in the middle of a capture, and sends only the product name, the key, a one-way hash of the Windows machine ID and a label made from that hash: no screenshot, file name, window title or setting. If the PC is offline, Pro keeps working for 30 days from the last good answer. The installer now shows a short license that says so, and the privacy page lists exactly what is sent. The update check is still the only other network call.
    • For Pro features to come: every Pro feature will show one sheet when Pro is locked ("Secure capture is part of A9 Snap Pro", with Get Pro, $14.99, Enter key and Not now), and a small Pro badge marks Pro features. No Pro feature ships in this version.

    0.5.2 · October 30, 2026#

    • Start at login now turns on for the Microsoft Store version. A new Store install started with the switch off (the startup task is declared off in the package and the app waited for you to switch it on), so after a restart nothing started it. It is now on for a new install, like the downloaded version, and the first launch switches Windows' startup task on; open A9 Snap once after installing. Switching it off in A9 Snap or in Windows (Settings, Apps, Startup) still works, and a policy that turned it off is shown, never overridden. The restart recovery starts the Store version through its app execution alias.
    • The strip shows up to 24 captures and you choose the row length. Captures in the strip is 6, 9, 12, 18 or 24; the new Per row is 4, 6, 8 or 12 (default 6, so 12 captures make two rows of six). A narrow screen gets fewer to a row, and the strip scrolls: with a search or a chip on, every match is reachable by wheel, arrow keys or PageUp / PageDown. The line under the chips says "48 captures" when all of them are in the list. Thumbnails load as they scroll in.
    • Search by app and window title. A9 Snap now notes the app and window each new capture came from (a clicked window, the window under the middle of a region, or the window you were working in for the whole screen), so typing chrome or part of a title finds it. Up to four app chips (Chrome, Outlook…) after Today, This week and Edited show the apps you capture most, and a card's tooltip says "App · title". The notes are kept in .a9snap\captures.json next to the edit originals; window titles can contain an email subject or a document's name, so they stay on this PC and never leave it. Remember which app each capture came from (Saving) turns it off, and Clear deletes what is noted. Captures from before 0.5.2 have no app.

    0.5.1 · October 29, 2026#

    • Search the recent captures strip. The strip still opens on your 12 newest, but a Search captures box at the top (press Ctrl+F or /) looks through every capture in your screenshots folder: type part of a file name, in any case, and the newest matches appear, with "Showing 12 of 48" when there are more. The Today, This week and Edited chips under the box narrow it further and combine with the text. Esc in the box clears it first; a second Esc closes the strip. Picking, copying, dragging, editing and deleting (with Undo) work the same on the results, and the search starts empty each time the strip opens. The box does not take the keyboard until you ask for it, so E, Enter, Delete and the arrow keys work on the cards as before.

    0.5.0 · October 29, 2026#

    • Alt+Z captures a window, the screen or a region. While the picker is up, the window under the pointer is outlined in Volt with its title and its size. Click to capture that window, cut from the frozen screen by its visible frame (no invisible resize border; a maximized window is the monitor's work area). Click the bare desktop, or press F, for the whole monitor the pointer is on (outlined and labelled Screen so you can see it first). Drag still takes a region, and the outline goes away once the drag passes 4 pixels. A window that spans two monitors is cut to the monitor you click on. Alt+X (OCR capture) uses the same picker.
    • A click no longer cancels. It takes the window under it, or the screen. Esc and right-click still cancel.
    • A key hint at the bottom of the monitor you are on: Click a window · Drag a region · F full screen · Esc cancel. It shows for the first 3 seconds of each picker, and the whole time for your first 5 captures after install.

    0.3.6 · October 27, 2026#

    • A9 Snap is packaged for the Microsoft Store. The release now also builds A9Snap_<version>_x64.msix, the same program in an MSIX package that Microsoft signs after it passes certification, so a Store install has no unknown-publisher warning and Windows does the updating. The installer from a9snap.com and its updater are unchanged.
    • The Store version behaves like a Store app. It makes no update check and shows no update controls (Updates and about says "Updates come from the Microsoft Store."); Start at login uses Windows' startup task, off until you switch it on, and the restart recovery from 0.3.2 works the same way; settings and the log live in the app's own folder (the manual says where), your screenshots stay in your screenshots folder. If the installer copy is installed too, the Store version tells you once that the two don't share settings.

    0.3.5 · October 27, 2026#

    • OCR capture copies text, not a picture. Press Alt+X, drag around text in the same pixel-precise picker, and the recognized words go on the clipboard as plain text. No PNG is saved and the capture numbering does not move. Text copied confirms it; No text found or a recognition problem leaves the clipboard alone. The hotkey is configurable in Shortcuts, with its own conflict warning, and the tray menu has an OCR capture item.
    • Copy text from an existing capture. Right-click a capture in the recent strip and choose Copy text, or use the editor's new Copy text button. The editor recognizes the picture currently on screen, including an unsaved crop or annotation, and stays open; neither path changes the capture or writes another file.
    • Recognition is completely local. A9 Snap uses Windows' built-in OCR and your installed OCR languages. Pictures go to the recognizer in memory only: no upload, no temporary image or text files, and no recognized text in the log.

    0.3.4 · October 27, 2026#

    • Delete a screenshot from Home. Point at a capture in Recent captures and a small red trash can appears in its corner (also when it has the keyboard focus; always visible on a touch screen). Click it, or press Delete on a focused capture: it fades out, the grid fills with the next-newest one, and a note at the bottom says "Deleted snag_0042.png · Undo" for 6 seconds. The file and, if you had edited it, its hidden original in .a9snap go to the Recycle Bin, never away for good, and Undo puts both back under the same names. If Windows can't move the file (another app has it open, say) the capture stays and the note says why.
    • The strip has the same. Delete takes the picked captures (or the focused one) to the Recycle Bin with one "Deleted 3 screenshots · Undo" note, a small trash can shows on a card under the pointer, and the right-click menu's Delete takes the whole selection a card belongs to.
    • A capture deleted from A9 Snap keeps its number while the app is running: deleting the newest one no longer hands its number to the next capture, so "Next file" stays where it was. (Files deleted in Explorer still follow the folder, as before.)

    0.3.3 · October 27, 2026#

    • The A9 Snap manual is at a9snap.com/docs: how to capture, the recent captures strip, the editor (and what Save, Save as new and Revert do to your files), every setting, Start at login, keyboard shortcuts, privacy, and what to do when something goes wrong. It has a search box, screenshots, and an "On this page" list on each page.
    • A Manual row in the Home window (under the section menu) opens it in your browser, and a ? button at the top right of every section opens the page for that section. F1 with the Home window in front does the same, and the tray menu has a Manual item above Settings.

    0.3.2 · October 27, 2026#

    • A9 Snap comes back after Windows closes it. When Windows closes every app for a Restart that never finishes (or is cancelled), no sign-in follows, so the login entry never fires and A9 Snap stayed gone. Now, when Windows ends the session, A9 Snap sets a one-time task in your own Task Scheduler to start it again two minutes later. If the PC really restarts, the login entry starts it and removes the task; every start removes it. A9 Snap never holds up a shutdown, and asks Windows to restart it after a crash.
    • The switch shows what Windows will really do, not just what was saved. If you turned A9 Snap off in Windows' Startup apps, Shortcuts keeps the switch on and says "Windows has turned this off in Startup apps." with a Fix in Windows button that opens that page. A9 Snap never switches Windows' own setting back on for you.
    • The login entry is checked on every launch. It is written with the program's path in quotes (the install folder has a space in it) and, if it is missing, unquoted, points at another copy (a dev build, an old path) or is out of date after an update, A9 Snap puts it right; if you switch Start at login off, it is removed.
    • The log now says how A9 Snap ended: quit from the tray, Windows ending the session (and whether the session is really ending), a crash, or a normal exit.
    • A launch at login goes straight to the tray: no window, no confirmation, the hotkeys ready. New installs have Start at login on; if you had turned it off, it stays off.

    0.3.1 · October 24, 2026#

    • A new Home window replaces the single scrolling Settings page. Double-click the tray icon (or pick Settings in the tray menu) and it opens on Home: a side column with Capture, Recent captures, Editor (opens your newest capture) and Folder buttons, each showing your own hotkey, and a menu of sections: Home, Shortcuts, Saving, Strip and editor, Notifications, Updates and about.
    • Home shows your six newest captures as thumbnails, with the file name, how long ago, and the Volt dot on edited ones; click one to open it in the editor. Under them: where the next file goes (and what it will be called) and what happens after a capture.
    • Every setting is still there, in the section it belongs to, with the values you already had. The window is 1000 × 680 (it can be made as small as 860 × 600, and then the right-hand side scrolls); the version and an update card with Update now stay in the side column.

    0.3.0 · October 23, 2026#

    • The editor: draw on a capture and send it. Open it from the recent captures strip (E, a double-click, or right-click → Edit), from the Edit button on the "Copied · …" confirmation, or after every capture (Settings, "Open the editor after every capture", off by default).
    • Tools, each with a key: Arrow (A), Box (B), Ellipse (E), Highlight (H), Text (T), Blur (U), Crop (C), and Select (V). Five colours (Nine Red, Volt, Ink, white, blue) and thin, medium or thick. Every shape stays editable until you save: select it, move it, drag its handles, change its colour, delete it. Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) go back through the whole session.
    • Blur pixelates the region for real: the saved file holds flat blocks, not the picture with a layer on top. Text is Geist with a soft dark outline so it reads on any background. Crop: drag a region, Enter applies, undo brings the whole picture back. Ctrl+wheel zooms, Ctrl+0 is 100 %.
    • Ctrl+S or Done saves over the same file, so the numbering never changes, and puts the result on the clipboard as the picture and as the file ("Saved · … · copied"). Ctrl+Shift+S saves as the next numbered file instead. Esc with unsaved changes asks in the window: Save, Discard or Cancel.
    • The untouched original is kept in a hidden .a9snap folder inside your screenshots folder, so Revert can put it back. The numbering ignores that folder. A capture that was edited shows a small Volt dot in the strip. The original still holds whatever a blur hid, so delete the .a9snap folder before you share the whole screenshots folder.
    • The confirmation toast now stays 3.5 seconds when it has an Edit button, and is a little wider.

    0.2.1 · October 23, 2026#

    • Recent captures: a Volt Copy to clipboard button across the top of the strip, so you can pick with the mouse and copy without reaching for Enter. It says what one click copies ("Copy to clipboard", "Copy 3 to clipboard") and is off, reading "Select captures to copy", until something is selected. It does what Enter does (one capture as the picture plus its file, several as files, oldest first), says "Copied 3 captures" and closes the strip. Enter and Ctrl+C still work, and the button never takes the keyboard from the cards.
    • Double-click the tray icon to open Settings (or bring it forward). Right-click still shows the menu; a single left click still shows it too, a moment later, after the double-click time has passed.

    0.2.0 · October 23, 2026#

    • Recent captures: press Alt+Shift+Z (or pick it in the tray menu) for a strip of your newest captures, newest first, from the screenshots folder (6, 9 or 12 of them: Settings, "Show in strip"). It opens at once with placeholders and the thumbnails fill in; they are kept in memory only, never written to disk.
    • Select with a click or the keys 1 to 9 (Ctrl+A for all, arrows and Space to walk and toggle), then Enter or Ctrl+C: one capture goes to the clipboard as the picture plus its file, several go as files, oldest first, so they paste in the order you took them. A toast says "Copied 4 captures".
    • Drag one capture, or the whole selection, out of the strip as files into Explorer, a browser chat box, Teams or Outlook. Right-click a card: Open, Show in folder, Copy path, Delete (to the Recycle Bin, with an Undo on the toast).
    • Esc, a click outside, or the hotkey again closes the strip without touching the clipboard.
    • Clicking the "Copied · …" toast after a capture opens the strip with that capture selected (Shift+click still shows the file in its folder).
    • Settings: a "Recent captures hotkey" row beside the capture hotkey, with the same warning in Settings and the tray when another app owns the key.

    0.1.1 · October 23, 2026#

    • Numbering continues an existing folder: the scan ignores case and treats -, _, a space or nothing as the same separator, and the next file copies the style of the highest-numbered one (SNAG-7094.png is followed by SNAG-7095.png, whatever prefix Settings says). The count is read from the folder on every capture; gaps are never filled and a file is never overwritten.
    • Settings shows the real next file name, read from the folder and refreshed as you change the folder or the prefix (it used to show a fixed example).
    • The crosshair, bullseye and loupe are drawn only on the monitor the pointer is on, also while you drag from one monitor to another.
    • The approved A9 Snap look: Ink and Graphite surfaces, Volt accent, the Bullseye icon (app, tray and installer) and the A9 Snap lockup in the Settings header, Geist and Geist Mono.
    • Dev releases are marked as pre-releases, so they never take GitHub's Latest badge.

    0.1.0 · October 23, 2026#

    • First release: press Alt+Z, drag a region (crosshair, bullseye and an 8x loupe help you land on a single pixel), and the picture is on your clipboard and saved as the next numbered PNG in your screenshots folder.
